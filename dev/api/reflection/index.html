Mutual irradiation (the reflection effect)

src/reflection.jl computes how each component of a close binary heats the facing side of the other, as a radiosity problem. It follows PHOEBE 2.5 (phoebe/lib/reflection.h, M. Horvat), restricted to two detached convex bodies.

It is a forward-model step: it takes both meshes and their intrinsic (gravity-darkened) temperature maps and returns heated maps. It is not part of the imaging optimiser and has no ChainRulesCore rules.

The model

For a pair of surface elements i (on one star) and j (on the other), separated by a = r_j − r_i with s = |a|, cosθ_i = n̂_i·â, cosθ_j = −n̂_j·â:

G[i,j]  = cosθ_i cosθ_j / (π s²)                         geometric (Lambert) kernel
F0[i←j] = A_j G[i,j]                                     Lambert view factor
F [i←j] = A_j G[i,j] · D_j(cosθ_j) / (D0_j/π)            limb-darkened view factor
D0/π    = 2 ∫₀¹ D(μ) μ dμ                                ⇒ F ≡ F0 for a uniform law

The D/(D0/π) factor conserves the energy emitted by each element; only its angular distribution changes.

Two solvers, selected by method:

Horvat:  S₀    = L_LD·F₀
         F_in  ← S₀ + L₀·diag(R)·F_in          (reflected light re-emitted Lambertian)
         F_out = F₀ + diag(R)·F_in
Wilson:  M     ← M₀ + diag(R)·L_LD·M           (limb-darkened at every order)
both:    T_new = T·(F_out/F₀)^(1/4),   F₀ = σT⁴

The albedo is indexed at the emitter (R_j) in Horvat and at the receiver (R_i) in Wilson. With a uniform bolometric limb-darkening law the two give the same result.

Bolometric limb darkening

ldbol1 / ldbol2 are NamedTuples (ldtype=, ld1=, ld2=) giving the bolometric law of each irradiator. They are separate from the passband ld1/ld2 that set ldmap.

ldtypelawD0/π
0uniform (Lambert), D ≡ 1 (default)1
1linear, 1 − x(1−μ)1 − x/3
2quadratic, 1 − x(1−μ) − y(1−μ)²1 − x/3 − y/6
3Hestroffer power, μ^x2/(x+2)

The quadratic law is the one used by PHOEBE, Claret & Bloemen, and Kervella et al. (2017) Eq. 5, so ld2 corresponds to the published b.

Usage

Both meshes must be in a common frame. create_binary_geometry records the secondary's sky-frame offset in center_offsets, which handle_reflection uses to place them. Stars built with create_star (both offsets [0,0,0]) raise an error.

star1, star2 = create_binary_geometry(tes1, params1, tes2, params2, bparams, tepoch_jd)
tmap1 = parametric_temperature_map(params1, star1)
tmap2 = parametric_temperature_map(params2, star2; secondary=true)
heated1, heated2 = handle_reflection(star1, tmap1, star2, tmap2;
                                     albedo1=0.6, albedo2=0.6, method=:horvat)

The kernels depend only on the geometry and the bolometric laws. For an albedo scan at a fixed epoch, build them once:

K = reflection_kernels(star1, star2)
for A in albedos
    h1, h2 = handle_reflection(star1, tmap1, star2, tmap2;
                               albedo1=A, albedo2=A, kernels=K)
end

Performance

The kernels are three dense n₁×n₂ matrices: 57 MB at 3072×768 in Float64, 28 MB with kernel_eltype = Float32 (sufficient; the mesh discretisation error is larger). Each solver sweep is a matrix–vector product; convergence takes 3–10 sweeps at typical albedos. crossbody_kernels is threaded; at 3072×768 it takes 6.8 ms on 1 thread, 2.1 ms on 32 threads, 1.1 ms on 32 threads in Float32.

Differences from PHOEBE

  • Two convex bodies only: no self-irradiation and no occlusion test (PHOEBE's two-body convex path, reflection.h:1166-1230, does the same). Contact systems are not supported.
  • Per-element (centroid) quantities, as in ROTIR's polygon Fourier transform. PHOEBE defaults to per-vertex.
  • Bolometric only; ROTIR's temperature maps are already Teff.
  • No redistribution of absorbed flux (PHOEBE 2.5 does not use it either). The fraction 1 − albedo is lost.
  • Mutual occultation is handled separately, by the occultation keyword of binary_cvis; see check_binary_overlap and occultation_weights.

Validation

test/test_reflection.jl covers the analytic limits (albedo 0, D → ∞, the substellar point for two spheres and its convergence with resolution), energy conservation, the D0 normalisation against numerical quadrature, and Horvat = Wilson for uniform limb darkening.

test/phoebe_crosscheck/ (not part of Pkg.test()) runs the same triangulation through this solver and through PHOEBE's C++ libphoebe.mesh_radiosity_problem_nbody_convex. On 1536 + 384 triangles at albedo 0.6, max|Δ(reflected)|/max is between 3×10⁻¹⁵ and 2.4×10⁻¹³ for both methods with uniform and linear bolometric limb darkening.

Gravity-darkening exponents

PHOEBE's gravb_bol is 4× ROTIR's beta. PHOEBE writes T = T_pole·((g/g_pole)^gravb_bol)^0.25 (von Zeipel: gravb_bol = 1); ROTIR writes T = tpole·(g/g_pole)^beta (von Zeipel: beta = 0.25).
