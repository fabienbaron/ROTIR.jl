Radial Regularizers — RADFLAT and RADIALVAR

Two regularisers that act on the projected geometry of the visible disk. Both use the same radial binning and act on the surface map without limb darkening.

Attribution

RADFLAT is an idea of Prof. John Monnier (University of Michigan), communicated privately (2026-07-01) and implemented in his SURFING reconstruction code. ROTIR's implementation works on a HEALPix tessellation rather than an image grid.

RADIALVAR is the tessellated counterpart of the "radialvar" regulariser in OITOOLS (radial_variance).

Purpose

On a non-rotating star observed at one epoch, a reconstruction can place dark or bright patches on the limb that trade off against the limb-darkening coefficient: χ² barely changes while the coefficient wanders. RADFLAT removes that freedom. Several epochs of a rotating star resample the limb and break the degeneracy without it; there RADFLAT suppresses real structure.

Definitions

radflat_bins sorts the visible tessels into nbins bins of projected radius ρ (default 6, as in SURFING).

RADFLAT flattens the azimuthally averaged radial profile:

f = scale · Σ_b w_b · (I_b/I_mean − 1)²,      w_b = ρ_b² · (projected area in bin b)

with I_b the mean brightness in bin b and I_mean the mean over the visible disk.

RADIALVAR removes the azimuthal scatter within each bin:

f = scale · Σ_b var_b / I_mean²,      var_b = (1/(n_b−1)) Σ_{j∈b} (x_j − x̄_b)²

The two are the between-bin and within-bin terms of the variance of the visible disk (one-way ANOVA):

Σ_j (x_j − x̄)²  =  Σ_b n_b (x̄_b − x̄)²   +   Σ_b Σ_{j∈b} (x_j − x̄_b)²
                    └──── RADFLAT ────┘       └──── RADIALVAR ────┘
                     between annuli               within annuli

test/test_radial_regularizers.jl checks this identity to machine precision. Each one alone leaves the other term free: on α Cen A, RADFLAT drives the radial profile rms to 1e-4 and raises the azimuthal scatter 17×; RADIALVAR does the reverse. Both together drive the map to a constant.

Usage

bins = radflat_bins(star; nbins = 6)          # from the projected geometry
regs = Any[["sobel2", 10.0, sobel_gradient_healpix(n), 1:length(x0)],
           ["radflat",   1.0, bins, bins.idx]]   # 4th element: bins.idx
x = image_reconstruct_oi(x0, data, stars; maxiter = 20000, regularizers = regs)

The pixel subset (4th element) must be the index set radflat_bins was built from; a length mismatch raises an error.

ROTIR.radflat_bins — Function
radflat_bins(star; nbins=6, subset=nothing) -> NamedTuple

Bin the visible tessels (or subset) by projected radius, for "radflat" and "radialvar". Pass the result as the third element of the regulariser entry and its idx as the fourth.

ρ is the distance of each tessel's centre (mean of its four projected corners) from the disk centre, normalised to 1 at the outermost tessel; the nbins bins are equal in ρ. Each bin's weight is w_b = ρ_b² · Σ polyflux over the bin, with ρ_b the bin centre.

Returns (idx, bin, nbins, w, count, rho). Warns when a bin is empty or holds fewer than 3 tessels; the innermost bin empties first, and nside ≥ 8 (tessellation_healpix(3)) is stable to about 1 %.

ROTIR.spheroid_radflat_fg — Function
spheroid_radflat_fg(x, g, bins; scale=1e5, verbose=false) -> f

RADFLAT (J. Monnier, priv. comm. 2026-07-01): penalise departures of the azimuthally averaged radial brightness profile from flat.

f = scale · Σ_b w_b · (I_b/I_mean − 1)²

I_b is the mean of x in radial bin b, I_mean the mean over all binned tessels, and bins comes from radflat_bins. x is the map without limb darkening, so the radial profile is left to the limb-darkening law. An empty bin contributes zero. Raises if x and bins have different lengths.

Intended for a non-rotating star observed at one epoch.

Gradient

With n_b tessels in bin b, N in total and r_b = I_b/I_mean,

∂f/∂x_j = 2·scale·[ w_{b(j)}(r_{b(j)} − 1)/(n_{b(j)}·I_mean)
                    − (1/(N·I_mean))·Σ_b w_b (r_b − 1) r_b ]

ROTIR.spheroid_radialvar_fg — Function
spheroid_radialvar_fg(x, g, bins; scale=1e5, normalize=true, verbose=false) -> f

RADIALVAR: penalise the azimuthal scatter of the map within each projected radial bin.

f = scale · Σ_b var_b / I_mean²,        var_b = (1/(n_b−1)) Σ_{j∈b} (x_j − x̄_b)²

Uses the binning of radflat_bins and, like spheroid_radflat_fg, the map without limb darkening. A bin with one tessel contributes zero. RADFLAT and RADIALVAR are the between-bin and within-bin terms of the variance of the map (one-way ANOVA decomposition).

This is the tessellated counterpart of OITOOLS' "radialvar" (radial_variance), which works on an image grid with elliptical annuli and sums raw variances. normalize = true divides by I_mean²; normalize = false gives OITOOLS' scaling.

Gradient

With c_b = 1/(n_b−1) and T = Σ_b c_b Σ_{j∈b}(x_j − x̄_b)²,

∂f/∂x_j = (2·scale / I_mean²) · [ c_{b(j)}(x_j − x̄_{b(j)}) − T/(N·I_mean) ]

The second term vanishes when normalize = false (and I_mean² is replaced by 1).

Measured behaviour

Benchmarks: demos/alphacen_ld.jl (α Cen A/B, no resolved surface structure, published limb darkening) and demos/rwcep_radflat.jl (RW Cep, asymmetric surface).

RADFLAT leaves the fit unchanged. On RW Cep with "tv2" off it takes the radial profile rms from 0.49 to 7e-4 while χ²ᵥ₂ goes 2.00 → 2.07, χ²ₜ₃ₐ 1.25 → 1.28, χ²ₜ₃ₚ 1.5 → 1.5, at every weight and "tv2" setting tried.

The recovered ld1 depends on the smoothing prior. RW Cep, parametric Hestroffer fit (V² + T3amp) ld1 = 1.58:

RADFLAT weightwith "tv2" @1e−4with "sobel" @1e1
1e−1—1.21 ± 0.23 (2.6σ), χ²ᵥ₂ 1.90
1e00.76 ± 0.38 (2.7σ), χ²ᵥ₂ 1.991.32 ± 0.11 (5.0σ), χ²ᵥ₂ 1.95
1e1—1.55 ± 0.11 (2.9σ), χ²ᵥ₂ 1.95

With "sobel" the coefficient approaches the parametric value as the weight rises; with "tv2" (k⁴ response, not scale- or resolution-normalised; see Image reconstruction) it does not. A flat profile leaves the model no radial freedom, so χ²(ld1) tends to the parametric curve and 1.55 against 1.58 is expected. What the reconstruction adds is the error bar (±0.11), which includes the azimuthal structure the map still carries (azimuthal rms 3.5).

orthoLD gave no reliable constraint. With "tv2" it gave ld1 = 1.95 ± 0.20 at 1e−3 (χ²ᵥ₂ 2.04), tightening to 2.05 ± 0.01 (36.6σ) at 1e−1 with χ²ᵥ₂ 5.93. With "sobel" it gave no constraint at any weight from 1e−3 to 1e1, with χ² unchanged (1.84–1.85). On α Cen its 43.5σ minimum was 41 % above the published coefficient.

RADIALVAR removes real structure. On RW Cep it raises χ²ₜ₃ₚ from 1.4 to 8.0 and χ²ᵥ₂ from 1.8 to 8.5, at three "tv2" weights. Surface features at assorted longitudes are azimuthal structure, which is what RADIALVAR penalises. Do not use it on a star with surface structure.

A reconstruction does not measure limb darkening. On α Cen, neither regulariser alone constrains ld1 (|a/σa| ≲ 1). Both together recover the published coefficient to 3.3 % (A) and 0.3 % (B), because the map is then constant and χ²(ld1) is the parametric curve; χ²ᵣ at the minimum equals the parametric value. Fit limb darkening parametrically ([`fitsphere_ld`](@ref)).

Convergence

Every number above is a difference of χ² between reconstructions, each of which must be converged. On RW Cep at tessellation_healpix(3), χ²/n changes by 0.14–0.66 between 300 and 3000 iterations, more than the spread across a limb-darkening scan. image_reconstruct_oi uses a tight gtol, so runs stop at maxiter; choose it from a convergence ladder.

Under-populated bins

Equal-ρ bins hold a number of tessels ∝ ρ dρ, so the innermost bin empties first. radflat_bins warns when a bin is empty or holds fewer than 3 tessels. nside ≥ 8 (tessellation_healpix(3)) is stable to about 1 %.

Effective map dimension

Each tessel is weighted by vis_weights × ldmap, and limb darkening suppresses the outer ones, so the data constrain fewer pixels than are visible, and the number depends on ld1:

ld1effective pixels (Σw)²/Σw²of 428 visible

At tessellation_healpix(3) (nside 8, 768 tessels) about half the visible map is unconstrained: ~185 of 428 directions have no measurable curvature, in Float32 and Float64 alike. An uncertainty on a coefficient that changes the effective dimension is therefore unreliable. A map coarse enough for the data to constrain all of it makes a joint fit of map, radius and limb darkening tractable, as in SURFING.
