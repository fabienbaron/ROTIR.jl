Parametric Fitting

A surface reconstruction needs the star's angular size first: the tessellation is built at a fixed radius, and the map cannot correct a wrong one. On one dataset a 55 % radius error gave χ²/n ≈ 4000 after reconstruction, against ≈ 4 at the correct radius.

This page covers stars whose surface has no structure beyond limb darkening. For a gravity-darkened rapid rotator, use fit_parametric (see Parametric Model & AD). Its gravity_law parameter selects von Zeipel (1924, gravity_law = 1) or Espinosa Lara & Rieutord (2011, A&A 533, A43; gravity_law = 2 or :elr). Gravity darkening and limb darkening both take flux out of the limb, so a law with too much equatorial cooling pushes the limb-darkening coefficient down, possibly below zero. See Choosing a gravity-darkening law and demos/gravity_law_comparison.jl.

The three functions

functionmodelfree parameters
parametric_chi2any surface_typenone; evaluates χ²
fit_sphere_ldround star, uniform surfaceradius, ld1 (+ld2)
fit_ellipsoid_ldtriaxial ellipsoid, uniform surfaceradius_x, radius_y, radius_z, inc, PA, ld1

Both fitters default to NLopt's Nelder–Mead (algorithm = :LN_NELDERMEAD; :LN_BOBYQA and :LN_SBPLX also work). method = :nautilus or :ultranest returns a posterior and log(Z); see Choosing the optimiser.

Fitting a round star

using ROTIR
data    = readoifits("RW_Cep.oifits")[1,1]     # readoifits returns Array{OIdata}(nwavbin, ntimebin)
tessels = tessellation_healpix(4)

fit = fit_sphere_ld([data], tessels; ldtype = 3, tepochs = [0.0])

fit.radius          # polar radius in mas; the angular diameter is 2*radius
fit.ld1             # Hestroffer exponent α for ldtype = 3
fit.chi2_per_datum
fit.params          # NamedTuple for create_star / create_star_multiepochs

fit.params is the input to the reconstruction. tpole (default 5000 K) only sets the flux scale, which cancels in normalised visibilities, so it is not fitted. The starting radius defaults to a quarter of λ/B_max.

Limb-darkening laws

ldtypelawld1default bounds
1linear, 1 − u(1−μ)u0 ≤ u ≤ 1
2quadratic, 1 − u(1−μ) − v(1−μ)²u; ld2 = v is fitted with fit_ld2 = true0 ≤ u ≤ 1, −1 ≤ v ≤ 1
3Hestroffer power law, μ^αα0 ≤ α ≤ 4

ld1 values are not comparable across laws. On RW Cep the linear fit stops at u = 1.0000, the physical limit (u > 1 gives negative intensity at the limb), with χ²/n = 6.53; the power law reaches α = 1.575 at χ²/n = 4.82. A coefficient at its bound means the law cannot express the limb darkening the data require.

Closure phases are excluded by default

Both fitters default to weights = [1, 1, 0]: V² and T3amp, not T3φ.

A centrosymmetric model (uniform disc, limb-darkened disc, uniform ellipsoid) has closure phases of 0° or 180° only. RW Cep's T3φ has an rms of 118°. Including T3φ in a symmetric fit biases the size and limb darkening and worsens the amplitude fits:

fitted onD (mas)αχ²v2/nχ²t3a/n
V² only3.001.808.66.1
V² + T3amp (default)2.931.588.95.4
V² + T3amp + T3φ2.550.8924.89.5

With T3φ, the diameter is 13 % smaller and the LD coefficient 44 % smaller, and the amplitude χ² is about 3× larger. The T3φ residual is the asymmetry the imaging step reconstructs. Use weights = [1,1,1] only for a star known to be symmetric.

Fitting a non-spherical star

fit = fit_ellipsoid_ld(data, tessels; tepochs = ts, rotation_period = 17.769426, ldtype = 3)
fit.radius_x, fit.radius_y, fit.radius_z, fit.inclination, fit.position_angle, fit.ld1
fit.equatorial_elongation, fit.polar_flattening, fit.mean_radius
fit.chi2, fit.chi2_sphere                            # compare: see below

The free parameters are the three semi-axes of surface_type = 1, so the fit can represent a tidally elongated star (radius_x > radius_y ≈ radius_z) as well as an oblate one. equatorial_elongation = radius_x/radius_y measures tidal distortion; polar_flattening = 1 − 2·radius_z/(radius_x+radius_y) measures rotational flattening. mean_radius is (radius_x·radius_y·radius_z)^(1/3).

Roettenbacher et al. find an equatorial/polar ratio of 1.06 with a different model. The 0.4 % difference between the two meshes is the discretisation error.

Several minima

Twelve starts on ζ And at nside 3 found three minima: the one above at χ²/n = 3.626, a pole-on one at 4.325 (radius_z at its upper bound, inc = 9°) and an edge-on one at 5.561. Four of the twelve, including the default start, ended pole-on with an equatorial/polar ratio of 0.41. restarts = 6 (default) runs Nelder–Mead from a grid of starting inclinations and position angles and keeps the best; restarts = 1 runs once from inc0 (default 60°) and pa0 (default 45°).

χ² improvement and shape

The ellipsoid lowers ζ And's χ²/n from 9.13 to 3.63, but at χ²/n = 3.6 the model is still incomplete, and V² and T3amp alone cannot separate surface brightness asymmetry from elongation. Synthetic data from a figure with equatorial/polar ratio 1.060 and three spots at ζ And's 20 % contrast return 1.072 (demos/zetand_triaxial.jl): spots bias the ratio by about 1 %. Shapes at the few-percent level are measurable here; tens of percent would be spot signal.

Uncertainties from Δχ² are not valid when χ²/n ≫ 1.

Rotation period and phase

A triaxial figure projects to a different ellipse at each rotation phase, which is what makes the three radii separately measurable. rotation_period (same units as tepochs) has no default and is required when the epochs differ; t0 sets phase zero. For a synchronised binary, set t0 to conjunction so that phase 0 faces the companion. With all epochs at one phase, the outline is one ellipse (three numbers for five shape and orientation parameters): the fit runs and warns that the radii are degenerate.

Uniform surface

fit_ellipsoid_ld holds the surface uniform. temperature_map_vonZeipel_ellipsoid uses g ∝ 1/r² with rpole = radius_x and is not validated. For a gravity-darkened oblate star use surface_type = 2 with fit_parametric; for a tidally distorted star in a close binary, surface_type = 3 (Roche lobe).

Degeneracies and checks

Pole-on (inc → 0), the projection is circular for any shape, and the radii, inc and PA are not separately identifiable. Bounds are inc ∈ [0°, 90°] and PA ∈ [0°, 180°]: 180° − inc and PA + 180° are almost indistinguishable (for a triaxial body, flipping the inclination reverses the direction the long axis sweeps with phase).

  • The radii and ld1 start from a fit_sphere_ld fit (seed_from_sphere = true).
  • The sphere is the equal-radii case, so the ellipsoid χ² cannot be higher; if it is, the function warns and chi2_sphere shows the comparison.
  • It warns when a parameter is within 0.1 % of the range from a bound, and when inc < 15°.

Choosing the optimiser

fit_sphere_ld(data, tessels; method = :neldermead)   # default, point estimate
fit_sphere_ld(data, tessels; method = :nautilus)     # posterior, uncertainties, log(Z); `using Nautilus`
fit_sphere_ld(data, tessels; method = :ultranest)    # the same through Python; `using PythonCall`

:nautilus is pure-Julia importance nested sampling. :ultranest runs UltraNest through PythonCall. Loading PythonCall invalidates OITOOLS' precompiled plotting code: one canvas build goes from 338 ms to 2477 ms.

Parallel tempering (Pigeons) is offered in the GUI's Model tab for sphere, ellipsoid and rapid-rotator fits, with using Pigeons, Distributions, LogDensityProblems, ADTypes, Zygote. Use it for multimodal posteriors: demos/rho_cas_basins.jl finds several minima between 2.2 and 3.7 mas diameter for one star. Check the round-trip count; with fewer than three, the chains did not cross the temperature ladder and the result covers one mode only.

The samplers use the same box prior as the optimiser bounds. The result has the same fields as the point estimate, with the posterior median as the value, plus:

fieldmeaning
radius_err, ld1_errhalf the 68 % interval (fit_sphere_ld only)
q16, q84the 68 % interval, per parameter
logz, logzerrlog evidence and its uncertainty
samplesposterior samples, nsamples × nfree

When to sample

  • Uncertainties. On a spotted star the surface can absorb part of the limb signal, so the limb-darkening coefficient may be much less constrained than the χ² minimum suggests.
  • Model comparison. fit_ellipsoid_ld contains fit_sphere_ld as a special case, but Δχ² is meaningful only when χ²/n ≈ 1. On RW Cep χ²/n = 4.4, and a Δχ² of about 1200 does not show oblateness. Compare log(Z) instead.

Cost

Nested sampling uses no gradient and its cost grows quickly with dimension: about 10⁴ likelihood evaluations for two parameters, many more for six. Each evaluation builds a geometry and a polygon Fourier transform; use nside 3 for sampling runs.

For UltraNest, use_stepsampler defaults to false for the sphere and true for the ellipsoid, and min_num_live_points = 400. Nautilus evaluates likelihoods in parallel (threaded = true).

Cross-check with `:neldermead`

The optimiser returns the χ² minimum and the samplers the posterior median; for a skewed posterior these differ. They should agree within a fraction of the posterior width. A poorly chosen prior gives a narrow posterior around a wrong value.

Evaluating χ² without fitting

parametric_chi2(params, tessels, data, tepochs; weights = [1,1,0], uniform = true)

weights defaults to (1, 1, 1). uniform = true uses a uniform surface instead of parametric_temperature_map (identical for surface_type = 0).

Next

Image Reconstruction recovers the surface structure left in the residuals (on RW Cep, a residual χ²t3φ/n of several hundred).

ROTIR.parametric_chi2 — Function
parametric_chi2(params, tessels, data, tepochs; weights=[1,1,1]) -> χ²

χ² of a purely parametric star: build the geometry, generate its map from params alone, and compare to data. No surface reconstruction and no regularizer are involved.

weights scales [V², T3amp, T3φ]. The default weights everything equally; see fit_sphere_ld for why you usually do not want T3φ in a symmetric fit.

uniform = true forces a UNIFORM surface brightness (tpole everywhere) instead of calling parametric_temperature_map, so the only structure left is limb darkening. For surface_type = 0 that is what the map already is, so it changes nothing; for the ellipsoid it matters, because temperature_map_vonZeipel_ellipsoid is a placeholder gravity model (g ∝ 1/r², with an unverified rpole = radius_x) that you do not want silently folded into a shape fit.

ROTIR.fit_sphere_ld — Function
fit_sphere_ld(data, tessels; kwargs...) -> NamedTuple

Fit a round star with a uniform surface, limb darkening only — the simplest parametric model there is, and the right starting point for a non-rotating star (red supergiant, Cepheid) before snapshot imaging.

Free parameters: radius (mas, POLAR radius = angular diameter / 2) and the limb-darkening coefficients ld1 (and ld2 for ldtype = 2). tpole only sets the flux scale, which cancels in the normalised visibilities, so it is held fixed.

Why weights = [1, 1, 0] by default — T3φ is excluded

Any centrosymmetric model produces closure phases of exactly 0° or 180°. A spotted star does not: RW Cep's T3φ has an rms of 118°. Including T3φ therefore does not improve the fit, it drags the symmetric model toward a compromise that is worse in the amplitudes. Measured on RW Cep, fitting a power-law LD disc:

fitted onD (mas)αχ²v2/nχ²t3a/n
V² only3.001.808.66.1
V² + T3amp2.931.588.95.4
V² + T3amp + T3φ2.560.9424.89.5

The large residual T3φ is not a failure — it is the asymmetry, and it is what the imaging step then reconstructs. Pass weights = [1,1,1] only if you believe the star is symmetric.

Accuracy

Validated against OITOOLS' analytic visibility_ldpow on RW Cep: at HEALPix nside 5 the fitted diameter agrees to 0.003 % and α to 0.07 %; at nside 3, to 0.12 % and 1.2 %. The discretisation-induced χ² is negligible at every resolution (rms ΔV² = 3.3e-4 at nside 3, against typical error bars of 2e-3).

Arguments

  • ldtype — 1 linear 1−u(1−μ), 2 quadratic, 3 Hestroffer μ^α (default 3)
  • radius0, ld0 — starting guesses; radius0 defaults to half the first-null estimate
  • radius_bounds, ld_bounds — box constraints
  • fit_ld2 — also fit ld2 (only meaningful for ldtype = 2)
  • algorithm — any derivative-free NLopt algorithm; :LN_NELDERMEAD (default), :LN_BOBYQA and :LN_SBPLX are all sensible here

Returns (; radius, ld1, ld2, chi2, chi2_per_datum, params, status), where params is the NamedTuple ready to hand to create_star / create_star_multiepochs.

ROTIR.fit_ellipsoid_ld — Function
fit_ellipsoid_ld(data, tessels; kwargs...) -> NamedTuple

Fit a triaxial ellipsoid with a uniform surface and limb darkening: the model after fit_sphere_ld for a star that is non-circular on the sky, without a gravity-darkening law.

Free parameters: the body-frame semi-axes radius_x, radius_y, radius_z (mas), inclination and position angle (degrees), and ld1; the layout of ellipsoid_param_names. The keywords req0, f0, req_bounds and f_bounds are refused.

Keywords

  • tepochs (zeros), rotation_period, t0 (0): the phase is ψ = 2π(t - t0)/P. rotation_period is required when the epochs differ. For a synchronised binary, set t0 to conjunction so phase 0 faces the companion; otherwise only the axis ratios are meaningful.
  • ldtype (3), tpole (5000)
  • r0, inc0, pa0 (45), ld0, seed_from_sphere (true): starting point
  • r_bounds ((0.05, 20)), inc_bounds ((0, 90)), pa_bounds ((0, 180)), ld_bounds ((0, 4) for ldtype = 3, else (0, 1))
  • weights ([1, 1, 0]): V², T3amp, T3φ
  • method: :neldermead (default), :ultranest or :nautilus
  • algorithm (:LN_NELDERMEAD), restarts (6), maxeval (6000), xtol_rel, ftol_rel
  • min_num_live_points (400), use_stepsampler (true), threaded (true): samplers
  • verbose (false)

Returns a NamedTuple: radius_x, radius_y, radius_z, inclination, position_angle, ld1, equatorial_elongation (radius_x/radius_y), polar_flattening (1 - 2radius_z/(radius_x + radius_y)), mean_radius (geometric mean), rotation_period, phases, chi2, chi2_per_datum, chi2_sphere, params, status; with a sampler also logz, logzerr, samples, q16, q84 (the point values are then posterior medians).

restarts starts from several orientations and keeps the lowest χ²; this objective has several minima (three on ζ And at HEALPix 3). restarts = 1 runs once from inc0/pa0.

From a single rotation phase the projected outline is one ellipse (three numbers) against five shape and orientation parameters: the fit runs and warns.

The surface is uniform (uniform = true in parametric_chi2), not gravity-darkened. For a gravity-darkened rotating star use surface_type = 2 and fit_parametric; for a tidally distorted star in a close binary, surface_type = 3 (Roche lobe).

Near pole-on (inc → 0) the projection is circular for any shape, and the radii, inc and PA are not separately constrained; a nearly spherical star leaves PA free. A figure at 180° − inc or PA + 180° is nearly indistinguishable, hence the bounds. Compare each radius with its uncertainty before quoting an orientation.

A uniform ellipsoid predicts closure phases of 0° or 180° only, hence weights = [1, 1, 0]. On a spotted star the T3φ residual is the spot signal. On a synthetic spotted star like ζ And (6 % distortion, 0.14 mas; 20 % surface contrast), the spots bias the fitted axis ratio by about 1 % (demos/zetand_triaxial.jl).
